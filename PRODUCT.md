# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Organizers ("Race Office" operators) — the host/promoter accounts that run
horse-racing championships on the platform (a B2B2C tournament system, one
organizer = one organization). They work at a desk in focused operational
sessions: creating tournaments, vetting horse/jockey registrations, hiring
referees, scheduling rounds, and ratifying/publishing race results. Adjacent
roles each have their own workspace: platform Admins (governance), Referees,
Owners, Jockeys. The organizer's job is operational throughput — move a
championship from draft → approved → run → official results without friction
or ambiguity.

## Product Purpose

The Organizer workspace is the operations console for hosting professional
tournaments under the platform's 3-gate moderation model (onboarding,
tournament approval, result confirmation). Success = an organizer can run a
full championship lifecycle confidently from one place, always knowing the
current state and the next action. It is a working tool, not a showroom.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic but operational — a "Race Office" carrying the gravitas of
professional horse racing (charcoal + brass/gold, serif display headlines)
applied to a calm, legible admin surface. Voice: confident, precise,
understated. It should feel like the back-of-house control room of a
prestigious racecourse — not a betting site, not a generic SaaS panel.
Three words: composed, authoritative, legible.

## Anti-references

- The public/spectator site ("Night at the Races" marketing surface) — the
  workspace must read as a distinct operational tool, not a re-skinned
  consumer page.
- Generic SaaS dashboards — identical icon+heading+text card grids, the
  hero-metric template.
- Decorative excess — glassmorphism, loud gradients, gratuitous motion that
  competes with the task.

## Design Principles

- **Workspace, not showroom.** Clarity and throughput over spectacle; every
  screen answers "what's the state, what's my next action".
- **Distinct from the stands.** The operator surface stays visually separate
  from the spectator/public experience.
- **Review, then act.** Consequential actions (confirm/publish results,
  approve registrations) happen on a reviewed surface, never behind a native
  browser popup.
- **State is always legible.** Tournament phase, contract state, and result
  stage are shown plainly with a consistent status tone.
- **Restraint in motion.** Motion clarifies a state change; it never decorates.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Visible focus rings (already present), keyboard-operable controls,
`prefers-reduced-motion` honored, body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (watch muted ink on
tinted cream surfaces), and status never conveyed by color alone — always
paired with a label or icon.
